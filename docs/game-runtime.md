# Development runtime

The development runtime (`chase-game`) is the first part of the future game, and it is an early test program. **It is not the game yet.** It starts with the screens of the original game, from the logos to a new game, and then lets you walk the main character around a level, with behaviour measured in the original. Only parts of the first three missions (1-1 to 1-3) run. There are no enemies yet, and of the menus only the pause menu wheel works.

You need the game files first. See [Extracting game data](extracting-game-data.md) and [Building the tools](building.md). The program needs libepoxy and FFmpeg.

## Start it

Run this from the project folder, the one that contains `README.md`:

```sh
build/chase-game
```

A window opens and shows the start of the game, as it looks on the 3DS: the top screen above the bottom screen. To quit, close the window or use Quit game in the [PC settings menu](controls.md#pc-settings-menu); Escape never closes the game ([Escape](controls.md#escape)). With `--window WIDTHxHEIGHT` you choose the window size, for example `--window 800x960`.

To skip the screens and go straight to a level, use `--gameplay` (police station) or `--level` and the level's path (see [Other levels](#other-levels)).

## The screens before the level

The program shows these screens in the original order:

1. A short loading page ("Preparing Game Card", "Preparing save data").
2. The company logos, then the TT Games movie.
3. The language list. Push up or down, then press A.
4. The StreetPass question. A is Yes, B is No. Then the HOME Button notice (press A).
5. The autosave notice (press A).
6. The slot list. Push up or down, then press A on a slot.
7. "Saving Game", a loading screen with a tip, and the intro movie with its subtitles.
8. The "Doughnut Disaster" title card. Press A, or wait. Then the level starts.

The buttons are the keys of [Controls](controls.md): A, B, and the up and down keys. `--language 1-9` starts with another language (the game's language numbers: 1 English, 2 French, 3 Italian, 4 German, 5 Spanish, 6 Dutch, 7 Danish, 8 Portuguese, 9 Russian; the language screen lists Danish before Dutch and Russian before Portuguese). `--returning` starts as a player who has played before: the language screen is skipped and a button press skips the TT Games movie (the intro movie cannot be skipped).

What is not like the original yet:

- Several parts are stand-ins: how long the loading pages last, the spinning badge on the loading screen (a flat picture here), the picture behind the title card, the tip on the loading screen (always the same one), and the 3D scene that the original plays on the top screen under the title card (here the top screen stays black).
- Without `--front-save`, every slot is an empty "New Game" slot. With `--front-save savegame.dat` the slot list shows the slots of your save, and A on a used slot continues in the level that slot was last in (see [commands](commands.md)). The delete page is not done. To skip the screens and start from a save slot directly, run `chase-game --save savegame.dat --slot 0`.
- The game saves when the original saves (save points of the story, a collected super brick) into `~/.local/share/chase/savegame.dat` (`$XDG_DATA_HOME/chase/savegame.dat` when that is set), or into the file of `--save-out`. The slot chosen on the slot list is the one written. Start again from it with `chase-game --save ~/.local/share/chase/savegame.dat --slot 0` or show it in the slot list with `--front-save`. The save holds the progress, the play time, the studs and the super bricks; what was built or collected inside a level is not saved yet.
- The StreetPass pages act as if StreetPass were available, as it was in the comparison runs.
- The level after "New Game" is the street of Cherry Tree Hills, with the character at the position the original starts him. Mission 1-1 starts there: the cutscene plays, then the first light column (at the donut shop) is switched on.

## Keys

| Action | Keys |
|---|---|
| Walk, run and sprint | Arrow keys, or W, A, S and D |
| Walk slowly | Hold Left Alt |
| Jump | Q, or the right mouse button |
| Punch (the Y button) | R, a short press |
| Turn the camera | Z (left) and C (right). Pushing left or right also turns the camera. |
| Pause menu | Return, or Keypad Enter with PC controls on; Escape in both |
| Quit | Quit game in the PC settings menu, or close the window |

- The character runs when you push the keys. After about one second of running, the character sprints.
- The keys come from `config/controls.cfg`. See [Controls](controls.md).
- F3 shows or hides the [debug overlay](debug-overlay.md) (frame rate, memory, CPU and a few level values). It is off by default.
- In a level the game updates 30 times per second, every 1/30 s, like the original in the driven police car. Other screens still follow the display rate.

## What works

- The speeds, the walk, run and sprint change, and the turning follow the original game.
- The camera follows behind the character, as in the original.
- The sky is drawn: the level's sky dome (clouds and the skyline gradient) follows the camera and rises a little when the character is high above the sea, as in the original. Where the dome ends below the horizon the plain background colour shows. The depth the original gives the sky is a stand-in (the sky is always behind everything), and a level with two sky objects uses the first.
- Shadows are drawn from the level's shadow light, as in the original: the player, the level characters and the objects whose class casts a shadow darken the ground of the street and the objects that receive one, within 15 m around a point ahead of the camera. The shadow has hard edges where the original's are soft, and it also darkens fog and see-through things drawn over it. People walking in the street and traffic cast no shadow yet. In the scanner view there are no shadows.
- Models are drawn in the original's order: solid parts first, then see-through parts from the farthest to the nearest, so glass, light columns and fading objects overlap as in the original. Particle effects are still drawn after everything else.
- The waypoint light columns and their badges in the first level turn see-through when the camera comes close to them on its way to the character (within 8 units), as in the original, and fade back in when it moves away.
- In some places the camera is fixed, as in the original: the police station and every area the level marks with a fixed camera. There it does not turn when you press the camera buttons, and a crossed-out camera picture shows at the bottom left of the top screen while you press them. In the two areas the levels mark as camera blockers (one in Fort Meadows, one in Downtown) the camera stops where it was and stays there while the character is inside, as the original's code does; this was not compared with the running original. Details such as the smooth change between fixed cameras are not done yet.
- While a script runs a camera sequence (a camera that flies along a path), the character cannot be controlled: the stick and the buttons do nothing, and other characters stop too unless the script keeps them running. When the sequence ends you can move again at once. In mission 1-1 this is why X does not let you out of the police car for the first 9 seconds. Other effects of the sequence (hidden display, stopped clocks) are not done yet.
- The camera keeps out of the level's walls. Near a wall it moves closer to the character and slides to the side, as measured in the original.
- The floor and walls come from the level. The character climbs steps and kerbs, and slides along walls.
- Many objects of the level block the character and the camera like the original: lamp posts, traffic lights, benches, bins, crates, barriers, doors and signs. The character is stopped by them and slides along them.
- The level's other people stand where the level puts them, with their own heads and clothes. They do not talk or react, and they do not walk by themselves (the pedestrians below do). They play their idle animations, and the animations the mission scripts ask for (for example the van driver stuck in the mailbox in mission 1-1). A script that waits for such an animation waits until it has played, as in the original. Animations that scripts ask of the main character or of objects such as doors are not played yet.
- Pedestrians: in the street of Cherry Tree Hills, once the level's scripts switch traffic and pedestrians on, up to five pedestrians appear in front of the camera, fade in and walk along their paths with the walk animation. They change rooms as the streets load, and stop for vehicles. They make no sound yet.
- Traffic cars: the cars of the level's traffic (`Traffic01` and so on) appear on the roads around the player, where the original puts them, with the rules of the original's traffic manager: one new car at a time, 50 to 130 metres from the camera (the first cars of a level closer, up to 76 metres), never two on the same road within 16 metres, only on the roads of the level's junctions that scripts have not switched off (`EnableTrafficPath`), and the level's limit (ten cars in Cherry Tree Hills, set again by mission scripts). A car leaves again when it is further than 50 metres from the player and has been outside the camera's view for one to three seconds (a random time; a car you can see stays), and every car leaves when a script switches traffic off. The cars drive along their roads at 8 to 12 metres per second, as the original's traffic driver does: they stop behind a car or the player in front of them, stop at a red light of the junction ahead and go on when their light lets them pass, take a lane through the junction to a random open road out of it, and leave when their road leads into no junction. A braking or waiting car shows the red brake light of its rear and a glow at each rear light while it is in view; the light fades out over half a second after the car goes on. Each car has a physics body that follows it while it drives, as in the original. The player's car and the other cars with a physics body of the level collide with it; the car that is hit is switched to a free physics body, asks its driver for the original's knocked state and goes back to its road when it is slow and has had its wheels on the ground (not compared with the original in play yet). People do not push the traffic cars yet. The wheels of a driving car turn with its speed. The program prints a line for each car that appears or leaves, whose body joins or leaves the physics world, that is hit by a car (`traffic car NAME: hit, driver requests state 4 at update U`), that stops at or goes on from a light, or takes a junction lane ([command reference](commands.md#development-runtime)).
- Traffic lights: each junction with two or more light pairs runs the original's light cycle (green 6 seconds, yellow 2 seconds, all red 3 seconds, then the next pair turns green). Each traffic light shows the colour part of its pair (green, yellow or red) and follows the cycle. Every junction runs the cycle at every update, whatever room it is in; the original runs it only for junctions in certain rooms. The program prints a line for each change, for example `traffic junction 3: pair 0 yellow at update 181`.
- The character jumps, falls off ledges and lands as in the original, including the short pause after landing. Running uphill slows the character down. At some ledges the character first slides down a steep edge before it falls. Two details of that slide are not exact yet: how it ends and the short pause after landing from it.
- The character makes the sounds of the original: footsteps while walking, running and sprinting, a sound when jumping, and landing sounds that depend on how far the character fell. Picking up a stud plays its coin sound. Sounds come from the place where they happen and are quieter and panned to the side as in the original. See [Sound](#sound).
- In the level, the top screen shows the 3D view with the display of the original game on top of it, and the bottom screen shows the map. See [The display during play](#the-display-during-play).
- Doors that open by themselves swing open when the character comes close, wait while he stands there, and close again, with the same timing as the original. This works for the hinged doors and the sliding doors of the police station. Doors that a script has to open (for example the front doors in the street) stay shut, because no running script asks for it yet. Doors make their opening and closing sounds.
- The street level loads and unloads its rooms while you walk, by the rule found in the original: the room you stand in, its neighbours and up to three rooms ahead of the camera. Rooms that are not loaded are not drawn. The room you stand in is found from the room models' size, a stand-in. The loading time (9 updates) is the one measured for one room. Characters and coins of unloaded rooms are still drawn. Other levels show all their rooms. The program prints `room streaming:`, `room A -> B` and `rooms loaded:` lines.
- The level's own scripts run while you play, with the same timing rules as the original (a script that waits continues later, a script can wait for another one). Scripts start when the level starts, when the character walks into or out of some marked places of the level, and when a script switches an object. Events of the story and many other kinds of starts do not start scripts yet; the program prints `script trigger event without check` for those once. Doors answer the script commands that open and close them, but no running script sends one yet.
- The question box of the police station door works: walk into the door area (after the first mission is switched on) and the game stops with the box "If you are on a mission...". A (OK) moves the character to the station door, B (Cancel) closes it. The box is drawn from measurements of one picture, so sizes may differ slightly. Screen fades from scripts darken the picture over half a second.
- You can walk out of the police station and back in. Leaving: walk to the lobby exit. A page with "Continue (A)" appears. Press A and you stand in the street where the original puts you. Entering: walk into the door area (after the first mission is switched on), answer the box with A, and press A again on the page. You stand in the lobby. The page is a stand-in: the picture on the top screen is a plain background, not the original's key art. The camera after the change is not the original's yet. The street level needs `--call-script 1-1_MissionSkip` for the door, unless you start with `--save` from a save that is past the first mission.
- Scripts can walk the player: after you answer the police station box with A, the character walks into the station by itself, at a speed measured in the original, in a straight line (a stand-in: the original follows a path). Other placed people do not walk by script yet. The AI commands of the scripts (enable, ignore player, invulnerable, pursuit and so on) are accepted and remembered but have no visible effect.
- Waypoints (the light columns of the missions) work on foot: walk into an active one and the player stops for about half a second, then the mission script it names starts (for example the donut shop of mission 1-1 starts `1-1_VanBreakdown`) and the waypoint switches off. The dev log prints `waypoint started script NAME at update N`. Waypoints for vehicles work too: they fire when the vehicle you sit in (or jump into) reaches them. A vehicle waypoint that stops you (for example the shop of the donut van) brakes the vehicle until its script starts. A normal waypoint does nothing while you are in a vehicle and shows a hint text instead. The use-an-object waypoints do not work yet. A small waypoint shows its police badge turning and hopping inside the light column, as in the original. When a script switches a waypoint off, its column fades out as in the original and the badge goes at once.
- Gang spawners: characters that a level's spawner holds (the goons of the missions) are hidden from the start of the level. When a script switches the spawner on, it sends its characters out one after the other, with the original's waits between them, where the level places them; a second switch turns it off again. Once all of its characters were sent out and are beaten or arrested, the spawner starts the next step of the story (arresting works for the goons of mission 1-4, beating them does not yet). Each character gets the first order of its spawner when it comes out, as in the original: most goons stand idle, others run or jump to the spot the spawner names for them (in mission 1-4 the goons of the second and third wave jump to their places). Some first orders of the original are not done yet (the character then just stands). A standing goon notices Chase when he comes within its view distance and stands inside the goon's fight area (in mission 1-4 the showroom), and it gets ready to fight him, but it does not fight or move yet; at most six goons at a time are ready to fight, as in the original. A goon that stands at least 2 units from the place where it was set up waits about half a second and then walks back to that place with the game's path finder (at the port's stand-in walking speed) and stands idle there again; if a target comes in sight while it waits or walks, it gets ready to fight instead. A path the finder cannot find makes it try again after about a third to two thirds of a second. Spawners that should send goons out only when they are off screen do so at once. The program prints a line for each spawner change, each character sent out and the first order it got, and `ai conduct NAME: sees player1 at D, AI state 2 (not ported) at update U` when a goon notices Chase, and lines for the walk back (`ai conduct NAME: AI state 14 (walk back)`, `walks to its spawn point`, `walk ended, idle`) ([command reference](commands.md#development-runtime)).
- Build-its (piles of bricks the story switches on) can be built: stand next to them and hold A. One brick is built per press and it takes the same number of updates as the original; after the last brick the finished object appears and the story script that waits for it starts (for the donut van of mission 1-1 the script switches the broken van off and the driveable van on, and you can drive the van, see the vehicle entry below). The six bricks lie scattered around the pile and each flies to its place while it is built, then the finished object rises out of the pile. The character plays the building animation while you hold A, and a sound plays when each brick and the whole build is done. The bricks hop while you stand next to them (without their sound yet). The camera does not frame the build, and the studs and the saved state of a finished build-it are missing.
- Scripts can remove a prop (`DestroyProp`), fade an object in or out (`EffectFadeObject`) and wait for a room to load (`StreamRoomIn`). Far objects do not fade out with distance and models near the camera do not turn see-through yet. `SetPartyCharacter` only prints `party character ... swap not ported` when a script asks for another character than the one you play.
- Most script commands are not worked out yet. The first time a script uses one of them, the program prints `script function without handler` and the command's name, and the script goes on as if the command had finished.
- The coins (studs) of the level spin where the level puts them. Walk close to one and it is picked up. It flies up to the stud counter. After one second the counter goes up (silver 10, gold 100, blue 1000, purple 10000), shows the new total for a few seconds and slides away again. The program also prints the counter at the end of a `--script` run.

## Window mode and size

- `chase-game` starts in borderless full screen at the desktop size. With `--window WIDTHxHEIGHT` it starts in a window of that size instead, whatever the stored mode.
- **F11** switches between borderless full screen and a window. The choice is stored as `window_mode` (`windowed` or `fullscreen`) in `config/qol.cfg`, next to the controls file (`--config`). The file need not exist.
- `view_distance <factor>` in `config/qol.cfg` is the View distance of the [PC settings menu](controls.md#pc-settings-menu): one of `1.25`, `1.5`, `1.75` and so on in quarter steps up to `4.0`, then `5.0`, `6.0` and so on in whole steps up to `20.0` (`1.0` is also accepted). Without the line the factor is x1.0, the original. With `master Improved` or `master Custom` the far plane of every camera, the fog start and end and the pedestrians' spawn and fade distances are multiplied by it, and above x1.0 every room whose box comes within 260 m times the factor of the camera is loaded besides the rooms the game loads itself (they finish loading 9 updates after they come in range, also at the level start); `master Original` always uses x1.0. Traffic is not scaled yet (open item). Any other value stops the program with an error naming the line.
- `resolution WIDTHxHEIGHT` in `config/qol.cfg` sets the size of the window (not used in full screen). Without the line the window has the desktop size. A size larger than the largest display mode of the monitor is limited to it; another size that is not a display mode is used as written. A malformed line stops the program with an error.
- The window cannot be made smaller than 400x240: the window has this minimum from its creation, and a `--window` size below it stops the program with an error naming the option (the option parses widths from 200).
- The [PC settings menu](controls.md#pc-settings-menu) writes `config/qol.cfg` after each change of its Display and Quality of life tabs and the window follows at once (window mode and size). After a display change it asks "Keep these settings?"; without an answer within 15 seconds, or on Escape, the old window mode and size come back and are written again. A menu or file error stops the program with a message. A resolution chosen in the menu also resizes a window started with `--window`.
- `config/qol.cfg` is read at startup. With `master Improved` (or `widened_view on` under `master Custom`) the 3D view is widened over the whole window, the bottom screen is hidden with it, and only the gameplay camera is widened (cutscene, director and scanner views and movies keep the 5:3 picture with bars at the sides), the HUD parts sit at the corners of the widened view, or at the corners of the 5:3 picture while a cutscene, director or scanner camera shows it (hearts and portrait top left, studs and super bricks top right, camera lock bottom left); the head icon and the scanner overlay stay on the usual top-screen rectangle. The widened view also loads the rooms that only its wider sides reach (inside the 16:9 view, outside the 5:3 view, up to the far plane). While the view is widened the bottom screen is not drawn and a click there is no touch; the top picture is fitted 5:3 and centred in the window, with bars where needed. While the view is widened a round minimap (the original map canvas cropped to a disc, the original zoom, rotating with the camera, diameter one fifth of the window height) sits at the right edge of the window, under the studs and counters of the corner HUD with a small margin; it is not drawn in cutscenes and movies, while the scanner overlay shows, in a dialog box or speech box, on the scene change page or while the fast-travel map is open (the pause menu replaces the whole picture). The pages of the bottom screen (pause menu, map, scanner, build-it, award viewer) have no input then. In the front end while the view is widened the top picture fills the window and each page with bottom-screen content (the "Preparing" notice, language list, question and notice pages, autosave notice, slot page, "Saving Game", loading screen, mission card) is drawn as a 320x240 panel centred over the darkened picture, as large as fits keeping 4:3; the panel appears and disappears with its page, and the logos, the movies and the black moments between pages show no panel and an undimmed picture; the loading screen's panel has a small spinning save icon at the lower right of the tip box; the "PC Settings" bar of the slot page is clicked inside the panel. While a movie plays (the logo movie and the intro movie, in the front end and in a level) there is no panel: the movie fills the window at full brightness, and a subtitle is the original subtitle bubble drawn over the lower part of the movie. A left click on a row of the language list or the slot list selects that row and presses A, and a click on an A, B or Y picture of the page (the question and notice pages, the shortcut bar) presses that button; a click anywhere else on such a page does nothing. Pages without rows or buttons (logos, movies, loading, mission card) do not handle the click in the panel: it passes on as the bound mouse button (A by default), so it skips the fusion movie (not on a first run) and the mission card like the A key. `master Custom` with `widened_view off` keeps the bottom screen; the widened view is the one setting that hides it, in every preset (switching PC controls on or off never changes it). A missing file keeps the original view. `pc_controls on` under `master Custom` (and `master Improved`) makes the running game read the keys from `config/controls.pc.cfg`, see [PC controls](controls.md#pc-controls); the file is created from the built-in set when it is missing. With PC controls on, mouse X also turns the follow camera in play (see [PC controls](controls.md#pc-controls)): the cursor is captured while the game runs and released in the pause screen, in the PC settings menu and over the lower screen. While the player sits in a vehicle (and no pause screen or award viewer is open) the keys come from `config/controls.pc.vehicle.cfg` instead, and the runtime prints `PC vehicle controls on` and `PC vehicle controls off` at the switch.

## The display during play

- Top left of the top screen: the picture of the main character in its hexagon, and next to it four hearts. The heart at the end of the row beats slowly.
- The stud counter slides in below the hearts each time a stud is added. It shows the new total and a spinning coin in the colour of the last stud. The coin jumps for a moment when the total changes. After about two seconds the counter slides out again.
- The bottom screen shows the map of the level. In the levels the map is centred on the main character and turned so that the camera looks up; the arrow in the middle is the main character. In the police station the map keeps one fixed centre and angle, as in the original, and does not move or turn while you walk or turn the camera. The map buttons slide in as in the original: the characters button at the top left and, from mission 1-3 on, the scan button at the bottom left. A short click on the characters button does nothing yet; holding it for more than half a second opens the characters panel without its portraits. The other buttons do nothing yet.
- Not like the original yet: the hearts do not change because nothing can hurt the character yet. The map of each level is placed with the numbers of that level's map file, as in the original (the picture of the first level equals the original's; the other levels were not compared). In the police station the map of the first level is shown around the original's fixed centre and angle; the arrow of the character there is still turned as in the other levels, which may differ from the original. The map shows the marker of the active waypoint. When the waypoint is far away, the marker stays at the edge of the map and an arrow points to it. Mission targets (for example the police car of mission 1-1 once you reach its waypoint) show a small blue dot on the map. When none of a mission's targets is near enough to be on the map, a blue dot at their middle fades in over 1 second and stays at the edge with a blue arrow, and the target dots fade out; it fades away again when a target comes near. Enemy targets would use red and carry targets green, but nothing in the port adds those yet. Other mission marks and collectibles are not shown, and the map does not zoom yet. The display slides in a moment after the level starts; when the original does that is not known.

## Sound

- The program plays the sounds of the character and the studs through your normal audio output. If no audio device can be opened, it prints a note and runs without sound.
- A sound is louder when it is close and silent when it is far away. It is panned left or right depending on where it is as seen from the camera.
- The volume options are `--sound-volume 0-10` (default 10) and `--music-volume 0-10` (default 8). They are the game's option values, which the game divides by 10. Like the original, each level load then uses the volumes the save file holds for the slot that was saved last, so a run with `--save`, or with a save file that already has a saved slot, uses the saved volumes instead. A new game keeps the two options as the volumes of its slot.
- There is no music at the start of the police station or the street. The original starts none there either. Music that mission scripts start (the story script commands that choose a music track, or stop it) is played. Music that the original starts in fights or in vehicles is not played, because those parts are not worked out yet.
- Not played yet: the sounds of other people, animals, vehicles and objects of the level, the idle sounds of the character, and water or sand landing sounds.

## What does not work yet

- Carrying works for the donut box of mission 1-1 and the key of mission 1-3 (it lies near the garage once the detective clue has revealed it; stand close to it, within about one step, and press A). A green arrow floats over every carry object you can pick up, and while you carry it over the place it belongs to (the donut delivery spot, the keyhole); the arrow over the object fades while you stand where A would pick it up. The key is put into the keyhole with A; press A there again to turn it: Chase steps in front of the keyhole plate, turns to it and plays the original's key-turning animation, and about a second later (as in the original) the garage door opens with a camera on the door. The key itself does not turn yet. While the key switch can be used, a use marker (the spinning disc) shows on the ground in front of it, as at every lever. In the garage, two punches break the parts crate; the box it frees is carried (slowly, no jump) to the base plate and put down with A, which shows the generator parts to build (hold A). The generator switch built there is pulled with A: Chase steps to it, turns to it and plays the original's pulling animation, and the crane scene starts. For the donut box: stand next to it and press A to pick it up (the story script starts), press A far from the target to put it down (it goes back to its start after 5 seconds), press A inside the delivery area to place it into the target (the story script that ends the mission starts). The character turns to the box (or the target), bends and lifts it with the original's animations, and holds it in his hands while walking at the original's carrying speed. The moments where the box leaves the counter and the hands are taken from the animations; the pickup takes as long as its animation (measured); how long the put-down takes is still a copy of a measured value. There are no sounds. A put-down box appears under the hands (the original also drops it from the hands to the floor below) with the heading it had in the hands.
- Ziplines work: jump (B) under a zipline and Chase grabs it, hangs on and slides down to the lower end, then drops off with a small jump and falls to the ground, with the zipline sound. B while sliding drops off at once. A next to the high end of a zipline, facing it, also gets on (B there does not). Speed and timing come from the original (the slide is a little slower with `--script`, which steps 0.033 s instead of 1/30 s). The rope between the two ends is not drawn yet. Chase hangs from the item of his class (the police handcuffs) while he grips and slides, and lets go of it in the dismount; every item that a clip of the original puts in his hand (the magnifying glass, the scanner, the handcuffs, the tools of the other classes) is drawn the same way, hung on the hand bone the clip names. The program prints `hand item NAME shown|hidden at update N` when one appears or goes. How the items sit in the hand is not checked against the original, and the items of other characters are not drawn. The pole sounds are missing and the hold-on pose is the original's animation without any check against the original's picture.

## Other levels

Choose another level with `--level` and the level's path:

```sh
build/chase-game --level levels/levels/lvl_01_cherrytreehills/lvl_01_cherrytreehills_main.blvl
```

The path is the level's place inside the game archive, as in the example. Each level has a `<name>_main.blvl` file.

A new game in Cherry Tree Hills starts as in the original:

- The intro movie plays (once), then the first cutscene of mission 1-1 with the donut shop, then the first tip.
- Start (Return, or Keypad Enter with PC controls on; Escape in both) opens the pause menu of the original: the upper screen shows the collectibles panel (without its numbers yet), the lower screen the wheel with Resume, Options, Credits, Exit and PC Settings, Back and Select. The wheel turns and chooses as in the original, also by clicking or swiping on the lower screen ([controls](controls.md#pause-menu)). Resume, B and Start go back to the game; Escape is B on every page of the pause screen. Options opens the options wheel of the original: the sound effects and music volume, Invert camera (it turns the camera the other way with L and R) and Language. A change of a volume is heard at once; B after a change saves the options into the save file (without the original's "Checking save data" check) and goes back to the pause wheel. The Language row opens the original's grid of nine flag tiles with no flag chosen; a click on a flag chooses it, a second click or A stores that language in the save file and goes back to the options wheel, B goes back without a change, but the texts and pictures stay English. PC Settings opens the [PC settings menu](controls.md#pc-settings-menu) over the whole window; the game and the pause screen wait behind it (its Game tab holds only Quit game here) and the pause wheel is as it was after Escape. Credits and Exit do nothing yet, and the short black screen of the original before the menu appears is not there.
- In the police station, A on the award viewer's use marker opens the awards screen of the original ([controls](controls.md#award-viewer)): the lower screen has the wheel with Overview and the seven areas and the Back button, the upper screen counts the bronze, silver and gold awards of the save slot for the chosen area. The award trophies of the upper screen are not drawn yet, and the screen opens and closes without the original's fades. The program prints `award viewer opened at update N, bronze A/B, silver C/D, gold E/F` and `award viewer closed at update N`.
- Cutscenes fade the top screen to black before they start (when the script asks for it) and at their end, then fade the game picture back in. The fades come from the original's code: 0.5 s each, black quad over the top screen only. The short black pause before the picture returns is a stand-in measured in the original.
- The player is held during the cutscene. Add `--no-start-cutscenes` to skip the movie and the first cutscene while testing.

For the other options, such as `--screenshot` and `--frames`, see the [command reference](commands.md#development-runtime). Giving `--gameplay`, `--level`, `--script`, `--start`, `--particle` or `--save` also skips the front-end screens.

In-engine cutscenes that the level scripts start (for example the donut shop scene of mission 1-1) play in the level: the picture is the cutscene camera, the HUD is hidden and the player waits until it ends. A cutscene fades in from black at its start, also when a script blacked out the screen before it (as after the dog is released in mission 1-2). The cutscene camera follows the original's code: its moves between keys, its hard cuts and its field of view (from the camera's lens). Movies that a script starts play on the top screen. The faces of the actors and skipping are not done yet.
