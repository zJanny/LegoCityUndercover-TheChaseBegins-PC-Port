# Development runtime: what does not work yet

The parts of the original game that `chase-game` does not reproduce yet or only approximates. The [development runtime guide](game-runtime.md) says how to start it.

Back to the [documentation list](../README.md#more-documentation).

- Carrying works for the donut box of mission 1-1 and the key of mission 1-3. The key lies near the garage once the detective clue has revealed it. Stand close to the key, within about one step, and press A. A green arrow floats over every carry object you can pick up. It also floats over the place the object belongs to (the donut delivery spot, the keyhole) while you carry the object. The arrow over the object fades while you stand where A would pick it up. Put the key into the keyhole with A. Press A there again to turn it. Chase steps in front of the keyhole plate, turns to it and plays the original's key-turning animation. About a second later (as in the original) the garage door opens with a camera on the door. The key itself does not turn yet. While the key switch can be used, a use marker (the spinning disc) shows on the ground in front of it, as at every lever. In the garage, two punches break the parts crate. Carry the box it frees (slowly, no jump) to the base plate and put it down with A. This shows the generator parts to build (hold A). Pull the generator switch built there with A. Chase steps to it, turns to it and plays the original's pulling animation, and the crane scene starts. For the donut box, use A as follows. Next to the box, A picks it up (the story script starts). Far from the target, A puts it down (it goes back to its start after 5 seconds). Inside the delivery area, A places it into the target (the story script that ends the mission starts). The character turns to the box (or the target), bends and lifts it with the original's animations. He holds it in his hands while walking at the original's carrying speed. The moments where the box leaves the counter and the hands are taken from the animations; the pickup takes as long as its animation (measured); how long the put-down takes is still a copy of a measured value. There are no sounds. A put-down box appears under the hands with the heading it had in the hands. The original also drops it from the hands to the floor below.
- Ziplines work: jump (B) under a zipline and Chase grabs it, hangs on and slides down to the lower end. He drops off with a small jump and falls to the ground, with the zipline sound. B while sliding drops off at once. A next to the high end of a zipline, facing it, also gets on (B there does not). Speed and timing come from the original (the slide is a little slower with `--script`, which steps 0.033 s instead of 1/30 s). The rope between the two ends is not drawn yet. Chase hangs from the item of his class (the police handcuffs) while he grips and slides, and lets go of it in the dismount; every item that a clip of the original puts in his hand is drawn the same way, hung on the hand bone the clip names. These items are the magnifying glass, the scanner, the handcuffs and the tools of the other classes. The program prints `hand item NAME shown|hidden at update N` when one appears or goes. How the items sit in the hand is not checked against the original, and the items of other characters are not drawn. Poles and drain pipes work: walk into one (facing it, stick towards it) and Chase grabs it and snaps to it in 5 updates; the stick up climbs and down climbs down (the steps are the original's animation steps). A or B lets go (A drops, B jumps). At the top of a drain pipe Chase climbs over the edge. The pole sounds are missing and the hold-on pose is the original's animation without any check against the original's picture.
- Super build-its: on the police station helipad of mission 1-3 the original's panel shows the helipad and its price of 300 Super Bricks. It also shows "Not enough Super Bricks!" or "A Build". With enough bricks, A asks "Are you sure?" (A OK, B Cancel); OK plays the helipad movie, takes the bricks, shows the built helipad and starts the next part of the mission. The Super Bricks you pick up are counted, and the build takes 300 of them. While the panel is shown, the Super Brick counter (a spinning brick and the number) stays on the top screen. After the build it shows the lowered number for a short time. The two bricks beside the price are missing, and the panel's text is smaller than in the original. A built helipad is saved with the game and built again when the level loads; the scripts' reset and close messages reach the plate. The sound of the build plays if the plate has one.
- Fast travel pad: on the built helipad, press A on the pad beside the console. The player steps to the console and types, and the police helicopter lands on the helipad. The fast travel page of the bottom screen opens: the exit button (the arrow hexagon at the top centre) and six locked level buttons. The level map and the area name of that page are not drawn yet. Once the helicopter idles, click the exit button: the page closes, the helicopter takes off, you get control back, and the helicopter disappears after about three seconds. The pad can be used again after that. The question of a level button and the trip itself are not there yet.
- The punch works on foot: a short press of Y (R) punches when you let go. Chase turns to the nearest smashable prop in front of him and hits it (the dev log prints `attack: hit NAME (message 0, damage 1) at update N`). More presses near the end of a punch chain up to four. Timing and reach come from the original. A smashable prop loses health with every hit (the donut table of mission 1-2 needs five hits). A wobbling hit throws bricks and two studs; the last hit destroys the prop, throws more bricks (some of them super bricks; picking them up is described in the vehicle entry below) and starts what the prop is linked to: the destroyed table makes the bricks of the trampoline build-it available. Near an enemy of mission 1-4 (within about 3 m, in any direction) the press starts one of Chase's throws instead of a punch. The dev log prints `attack: fight move N on character NAME (state 227) at update N`. A wall, a prop or another person between Chase and the enemy stops the throw. The press punches instead. The enemy goes down: it lies for a moment and gets up again (the dev log prints `character NAME: state 227 MELEE PUTDOWN`, then `state 309 PUTDOWN STUN`, `arrest health 2 -> 1`, `state 313 GENERIC GETUP` and `state 1 IDLE`). A second throw makes an enemy of mission 1-4 arrestable: it lies for 4 seconds (`character NAME: arrestable`) and its CharacterArrestable script trigger starts (for two of the enemies that shows the arrest hint). A short press of A (Space) near the lying enemy arrests it, as in the original: Chase plays his cuffing clip and turns to the enemy (`arrest: player state 322 ARRESTING on character NAME`). The enemy is cuffed (`state 323 ARREST`, then `state 324 ARRESTED` and `event CharacterArrested`). One second later it is gone (`arrested (destroyed, disabled)`), which counts for its spawner. A press of A near an enemy that is not arrestable does nothing yet (the original grabs it). The cuffed enemy does not move as in the original and its cuffed idle clip does not loop. Neither Chase nor the enemy moves as in the original during the throw. The timing of the first throw matches the original. Other people are never hit. The wobble animation of the prop and the punch sounds are not done yet.
- Trampolines bounce Chase when he lands on them (for example after a jump): he takes off at the lowest speed, higher with every bounce while B is held, and keeps bouncing. Walking over a trampoline does nothing. After a few bounces with B held he rises high enough to grab the flag poles above the pad of mission 1-2 while he falls. The pad's own animation, the take-off animation and the trick bounces are not done yet.
- Climbing bricks work: jump (B) under a climbing brick and Chase grabs it and hangs on. Push the stick towards the wall to reach up and press B to jump to the next brick in that direction; A lets go. B on a brick with no other brick in reach jumps off. Timing comes from the original; jumps to the side, the jump over a top brick and the free climb walls are not checked against the original yet.
- Flag poles (acrobat poles) of mission 1-2: Chase swings on the pole. At the end of each swing he jumps to the next pole in the direction he faces. This needs B held, or pressed during the swing. Hold the circle pad towards the other side of the screen to turn him round first. Hold B while he turns to jump as soon as the turn ends. With no pole ahead he jumps off and falls; the last pole sends him to the landing spot. Timing and paths come from the original.
- Rails and climb bars of mission 1-2 work as far as the route needs: a jump (B) that meets a rail grabs it. Chase hangs on and moves along it with the circle pad. He goes round the corner rail onto the next rail. He lets go with A (falls) or with B (jumps, and grabs the rail again if he meets it). Speed and timing come from the original. A rail is also grabbed while he falls past it. Not done: narrow bars, crumbling bars, the `Ledge` variant, and the hang position of other characters. The `--script` keys cannot start on a rail, so only a real jump reaches one.
- Ladders work, including the crane ladder to the roof in mission 1-3: stand in front of one and press A, or walk into it with the stick towards it. Chase steps onto the ladder in 4 updates. The stick up climbs and down climbs down. At the top he climbs over the edge onto the roof. A lets go (he falls), B jumps off, and at the bottom he drops off. Speeds and timing come from the original. The climb makes a foot sound at each step, the climb over the edge a whoosh and an effort sound; they come from the sound tracks of the animations and are not yet compared with the original. Other animations with sound tracks (punches, brick grabbing, poles) make their sounds the same way, also not yet compared with the original. Not done: grabbing a ladder while jumping or falling. Not done: ladders that start rolled up. Not done: the wall at the roof edge (Chase can slide a little past it).
- Fences with the B prompt can be vaulted over, like the fence in mission 1-3 on the way to the push cart: stand near one and press B (jump). Instead of jumping, Chase steps in front of the fence and turns to it (0.1 s when he already faces it, else 0.2 s). He climbs over it and drops down on the other side. Near such a fence B never jumps, as in the original. The program prints `fence vault: NAME takes the B press at update U`, `fence vault: player state 20|326 at update U` and `fence vault: fall at X Y Z at update U`. Positions and timing match the original's at the mission 1-3 fence; Chase does not collide with anything during the vault.
- Pushable objects can be pushed, like the push cart of mission 1-3: walk into the cart from the side with its handle. Chase takes hold of it, steps to the handle and turns to the cart. While you hold the stick towards the cart he pushes it. Let go and he lets go. When the cart reaches the end of its track, the story script that waits for it starts. The program prints `pushable NAME: grabbed at HANDLE, player state 84 at update U`, `pushable NAME: player state N at update U` and `pushable NAME: state 1|2 at update U` (state 2 is the end). The cart is drawn where it has been pushed to, and `pushable NAME: drawn at X Y Z at update U` prints where it is drawn when the push ends. At the end of mission 1-3 the story script makes Chase jump from the bridge and fall to the ground; the program prints `player AI move: started|ended at update U ...` and, 60 updates later, where he stands. The height of that jump is an approximation of the original's. The gardener on the lawn mower of the rescue is drawn on the mower while it moves (`prop NAME: drawn at X Y Z in PARENT after it moved at update U` prints his position in the mower's frame). The cart does not stop at walls. The ledge platform that lifts Chase up to the cart in the original does not move.
- Mission 1-2, alley: after the bin scene the Doorlock script moves Holmes to the spot beside the dumpster where the original puts him. Moving other objects with the script is not done.
- Some mission targets get an arrow in the 3D view that bobs above them, as in the original. It is blue for places to go and red for people to catch. It fades in over half a second and fades out when you come within 4 units of the target's use marker. Its height above objects other than props and characters is not known yet. In the first missions the targets with arrows are still hidden in the port, so you will rarely see one.
- Mission 1-2, dog cage: the round use marker lies on the ground in front of the cage while the spot can be used. It is a slowly turning disc with coloured dots. Press A on it: the dog is released, the screen goes black, the cutscene fades in, and the mission end follows. The detective spots of mission 1-2 show the police-coloured disc too, until you use them. The other kinds of objects that show this marker in the original do not show it yet.
- The sound scanner of mission 1-2 works on the roof: stand on the scanner spot and press A. Chase takes the scanner out and the view switches to the scanner camera. Move the circle pad (or the D-pad) to look around, hold R to zoom in and L to zoom out. When the reticle stays on a target it locks; hold A to scan it. B leaves the scanner, and so does a click on the back-arrow button of the lower screen. The story scripts of the scanner run like in the original. The top screen shows the scanner frame, the zoom meter, the headphone icon, the reticle and the scan meter. The picture turns blue like in the original, without fog. Its glow is still missing, so it looks darker than the original. On the lower screen the characters button slides away and the headphone button (greyed) and the back-arrow button slide in; they slide back when the scanner closes. The tip pages the original shows on the lower screen during the scanner view (look around, zoom) are not shown yet.
- Getting into a vehicle works: stand within 2 units of an unlocked vehicle and press X (the E key by default). The character jumps in the way the original does (about 0.7 s, an arc to the seat) and is then hidden. Driving works: A accelerates, B brakes and reverses, the stick or the left and right D-pad steers (see [controls](controls.md#driving-a-vehicle)). The vehicle drives like the original. Its engine, its single forward gear and reverse, its steering and its tyres are the original's. The physics run in the original's fixed steps of 1/30 s. It falls with the original's gravity and rests on four sprung wheels. So it settles at the original's height and tilts with slopes. It bounces over kerbs, leans back when it speeds up and dips when it brakes. Braking hard in a turn locks the rear wheels like the hand brake. It collides with the street the way the original does: its body cannot sink into the street or kerbs, it lands with the original's bounce, and the edges of the street stop it. When it stands still for a moment it goes to sleep like the original (after about 0.75 s). It also hits the level's solid blocks for vehicles the way the original does. One example is the block about 75 m ahead of the parked police car of mission 1-1. The vehicle stops there, bounces back a little and keeps pushing while you hold A. It collides with other vehicles the way the original does: a parked vehicle you drive into is pushed away and stops again by itself. It smashes lamp posts, phone boxes, bus shelters and other breakable props that it drives into: they break into bricks in their own colours and a puff of dust, as in the original. The vehicle does not slow down, as in the original. A broken prop also throws super bricks (rainbow-coloured), and a hit above 10 m/s can throw one too. While you sit in a vehicle they fly to you (on foot nothing pulls them; walking over them is not checked yet), then to the super brick counter at the top left of the screen. About a second later each one adds its value to your super brick count and gives you 10 studs, as in the original. The pickup sound is not checked against the original yet. The screen shake, the flash and the sound of a breaking prop are not done yet. Other objects (barriers and props of other kinds) do not stop it at the sides yet, so the vehicle drives through them; a wheel treats the top of an object such as the helipad plate as floor. The character can walk through a vehicle that has moved from its parking place. The drawn wheels turn, the front wheels steer and all wheels move with the springs. Wheels of vehicles that have not been driven yet stay at their class-file pose, and they never come off in the distance. The camera is the original's vehicle camera: it sits about 15 units behind the vehicle and swings round to stay behind it. The stick does not turn it. The L and R buttons still do. Next to an unlocked vehicle a round X button appears above the character. It fades in and out. It is hidden when the camera is closer than 6 units to it. While you sit in the vehicle the HUD shows the vehicle's picture and blue hearts with the vehicle's health. A hit above 10 m/s throws six bricks in the vehicle's colours (at most one burst per second); they fly, bounce, lie still and blink before they vanish, as in the original. A hard hit (above 20 m/s) costs a heart: the vehicle flashes white, then pulses red for half a second, and smoke rises from its front. When the last heart is gone, or when you keep pushing the vehicle against something for 3 seconds without it moving, the vehicle breaks: its body disappears, the wheels fly off and bounce, and you cannot jump out. After 2 seconds the vehicle is back, whole, with full hearts, where it stood a moment before the break, and you still sit in it. The break throws bricks like a breaking prop. The sounds of the break and the fade of the screen at the return are not done yet. The sounds and the horn are missing. Press X again to jump out next to the vehicle; it brakes to a stop by itself. In mission 1-1 the police car and the donut van stay locked until the story unlocks them. The story script that waits for the police car to be entered starts when you get in. It holds the controls for about 9 seconds. The development options `--call-script 1-1_VanBreakdown --call-script 1-1_Get_Car` unlock the police car for testing. `--call-script 1-1_SO_VanFixed` makes the donut van drivable and starts `1-1_Van_Waypoint_Enter` (CTest `game-van-escort` drives it to the shop waypoint, where the story script jumps the character out).
- Only some kinds of objects block the character and the camera so far. People, animals and some special objects do not. The helipad of mission 1-3 blocks: its plate before the build and the built helipad after it, and objects that other objects contain (nested objects) follow the same rules; the parts of such objects that scripts switch on or off at the start are not applied yet, so some of them have no collision. Objects that the original switches off at the start (mission items, build-it parts and their results) are switched off by the original's rules. Nothing switches them on later.
- Objects stay as they are at the start of the level, except what is described above: doors, smashable props, build-its, vehicles and the dog of mission 1-2 move or break. Doors open only when the character comes close. An open door keeps the same blocking box as a closed one here. One measurement in the original differed, and the reason is not known yet.
- When the camera is stuck behind a wall for a longer time, the original places it again. That is not done here.
- Other characters do not block the character. Parked vehicles do: the character stops at them.
- The dog of mission 1-2, the cats on the roofs and the crow of mission 1-3 stand still where the level puts them. When you free the dog, it jumps out of its cage to Chase as in the original. The pigs, the sharks and the eight line-up characters of the police station are not shown yet.
- Props that the level marks as rocking sway gently, as in the original: the crane girder of mission 1-3 tilts by up to 2 degrees one way and 2.5 degrees the other. How fast it sways is a guess for now. A script command stops or starts it; the program prints `prop NAME: rocking off at update N` (or `on`). Moving platforms are not shown moving. A script that waits for one waits as long as the platform would travel at its speed. The crane platform of the generator scene takes about 3.4 seconds, as in the original.
- Mission 1-4: the roof scene's slow motion slows the whole game (scripts, animation, movement) to one clock tick per update for a few seconds. It holds all other sounds while it runs and plays the slow-motion sounds. The program prints `bullet time sound voices paused` / `resumed at update U`, `bullet time sound ID at update U`, `bullet time sound ID stopped over F s at update U` and `time scale S at update U` when the slowing or the return to normal speed is complete. The return to normal speed can ask the camera for another mode; the program prints `bullet time camera mode MODE requested at update U` and `camera mode A -> B at update U` when the camera switches. Only the normal camera is shown. At the end of the skylight scene the booster chaser comes out of his spawner and jumps into the stolen car, as in the original; other characters get into and out of vehicles by script in the same way. The program prints `character NAME: vehicle VEHICLE jump in started|entered|exiting|left at update U` and, when one is out again, `character NAME: left vehicle VEHICLE at X Y Z at update U`. In the chase the stolen car drives along its route `1-4_Vehicle_Path` at up to 29 m/s on its physics body. The original's AI driver steers it. The program prints `vehicle NAME: driver state N at update U` when its driver changes state. Its speed and line are not checked against the original. Each hit of the player's vehicle on it above 3 m/s takes one of its five hearts and slows it. The program prints `vehicle NAME: heart lost (S m/s), N left at update U`. Hits must be at least 128 updates apart. The fifth hit requests its driver state 6 (`driver state 6 requested at update U`). The chase ends, `1-4_Car_Crash` starts and the booster chaser leaves the car. When the chase starts the program prints `target hearts NAME: H of M shown, portrait PATH at update U (not drawn, SCRIPT-455)`: the portrait and hearts of the chased car are known but not drawn yet. Entering or leaving a vehicle and the end of a chase also raise their event for the level's own triggers. As in the original, the event has no object name.
- Particle objects of the level (class `particles`) start their effect when a script triggers them. One example is the effect of the goon's ladder kick on the showroom roof. A gang member who moves under the AI and then lies in the air inside a switched-on FallArrest bound is marked as arrested on landing. The program prints `character NAME: falls into fall arrest bound B, auto arrested at update U`. A gang member that Chase throws is not moved by the throw yet, so it does not leave the roof. The fall after a throw is ready for when it does: a thrown member in the air flies on and falls until it lands, and the program prints `character NAME: thrown fall started at update U`, `thrown fall ended at update U` and, for a marked one, `lands auto arrested, state 310 requested at update U`. The program also prints `particle objects: N, fall arrest bounds: M` at the level start and `particle object NAME: effect N started at update U` (`refused`, `stopped over F s`, `disabled`). Effects that the original starts by themselves, such as smoke and the lawn mower's grass, do not run yet. When the skylights break, the script's burst of bricks is spawned at the skylight; the program prints `debris burst at NAME: N bricks at update U`.
- Level scripts can start particle effects at an object (the smoke when the crane girder of mission 1-3 drops); the program prints `particles N at NAME started at update U` (or `refused` when the effect cannot start). The effect starts where the level placed the object, also when that object should have moved.
- Characters of the level that a script makes walk or jump move on screen. One example is the girder worker of mission 1-3, who jumps down after the crane scene. A character that walks stays on the ground under it (a slope up or down); where no ground is close below, it keeps its height. A script can turn a jumping character to face something before it lands, as in the girder worker's jump in the crane scene. Then he stops moving sideways and falls to the ground; the program prints `character NAME: released flight landed at X Y Z at update U`. The height of his fall is an approximation of the original's.
- A character that a level script attaches to an object moves and turns with that object: the girder worker of mission 1-3 stands on the crane girder and sways with it. The program prints `character NAME: attached to OBJECT at update N`. Objects attached to other objects (the crow on the girder, the girder on the crane platform) move and turn with their parent; the program prints `prop NAME: attached to OBJECT at update N`. The rope on the girder, the lawn mower smoke and the minecart smoke do not follow yet. Props that a script hangs on a bone of a character follow that bone while the character moves: the TV crew of mission 1-3 holds its cameras and booms in the hand (`weaponright`); the program prints `prop NAME: attached to bone BONE of CHARACTER at update N`. Their pose was not compared with the original yet, and a prop on a bone is only handled when the holder is a level character.
- Moving platforms that a script starts travel to their target and take the objects attached to them along: the crane of mission 1-3 lowers its girder in the crane scene. Platforms that follow a path, like the lawn mowers of mission 1-3, drive along it and turn with it, and the gardener rides along. The program prints `platform NAME: travel ended in state S at X Y Z after N node moves at update U` when a travel ends and `platform NAME: lap ended at X Y Z after N node moves at update U` when a looping path platform completes a lap. Platforms without a path do not turn yet. Chase rides a moving platform he stands on, as on the ledge lift of mission 1-3. He turns with a platform that turns its riders (`orient` in the level data); the program prints `player: ground entity NAME at update U` when he steps onto a platform and `player: left ground entity NAME at update U` when he leaves it. A moving platform does not push or squash characters beside or below it yet, and other characters only ride when the level attaches them.
- Walking slowly off a ledge looks different from the original, which first plays a short step.
- Jumping onto something higher, or under a ceiling, may not match the original.
- The animations do not blend. Their speed is an estimate.
- Studs are stand-ins in some details. Studs that a mission or a script switches on stay hidden. The spin starts at its own time and the pictures of the studs, also the flying ones, are drawn without the original's blending. Studs that a script adds to the counter later (the original shows the old total first) are not done.
- The pickup distance (1.5 units) was measured in the original, not taken from the game's code.
